Section 1

Writing & Grammar — Answer key & explanations

Question 1 A1

Hi! name is Tom. I from Canada.

Answer: A — My / am
A1 target: possessive adjective "my" and first-person singular "am." Distractor D ("My / is") is the most common A1 error — students carry "is" from the third-person "name" into the pronoun "I."
Question 2 A2

She to the supermarket every Saturday, but last week she online instead.

Answer: A — goes / shopped
A2 target: present simple for habitual routine ("goes every Saturday") contrasted with simple past for a completed event ("shopped last week"). Distractor D is a tense-mixing error; Distractor B/C fail subject-verb agreement.
Question 3 B1

When I arrived at the station, the train already , so I had to wait an hour.

Answer: A — had / left
B1 target: past perfect ("had left") to express an event completed before another past event ("arrived"). The adverb "already" is a strong signal. Distractor C ("has / left") is a common B1 error — using present perfect where past perfect is required.
Question 4 B2

the neighbourhood having improved considerably, some long-term residents remain concerned the rising cost of living.

Answer: A — Despite / about
B2 target: "despite + gerund phrase" (concessive connector) and the fixed collocation "concerned about." Distractor B ("Although") requires a full finite clause, not a gerund; Distractor D ("In spite") is missing "of"; Distractor C ("Because of") reverses the logic entirely.
Section 2

Listening — Read-aloud scripts

Read at a natural, conversational pace. Pause briefly between sentences. Read each script twice before the student answers.

Script 1 — A1/A2 level 🎙 ~25 sec per reading
"Hey, Jake! Are you doing anything this weekend? Not much — why? I want to go to the park on Saturday for a picnic. Do you want to come? That sounds fun! What's the weather going to be like? The forecast says it'll be sunny in the morning, but there might be some clouds in the afternoon. Bring a jacket just in case. Great idea. I'll bring some sandwiches!"

Student question: What does Mia want to do this weekend, and what is the weather forecast?

Expected answer: Mia wants to go to the park for a picnic on Saturday. The forecast says it will be sunny in the morning but cloudy in the afternoon.
Accept any paraphrase that captures both the plan and the weather. Award partial marks if only one detail is correct.
Script 2 — B1/B2 level 🎙 ~40 sec per reading
"Welcome back. In today's feature, we're looking at a shift in our eating habits. A new survey shows that the number of people cooking a full meal from scratch at home has dropped by nearly a third over the past five years. Researchers point to increasingly long working hours as the primary cause — many adults simply feel they don't have the time or energy to cook after a full day at work. However, nutritionists argue this doesn't have to mean relying on expensive takeaways. One practical suggestion is batch cooking: spending two to three hours on a Sunday preparing several meals in advance, which can then be refrigerated or frozen and reheated quickly throughout the week."

Student question: What is the main reason people are cooking less at home, and what does the speaker suggest as a practical solution?

Expected answer: The main reason is that long working hours leave people with too little time and energy to cook. The speaker suggests batch cooking on Sundays — preparing multiple meals in advance to reheat during the week.
Award full marks for capturing both cause and solution. Award partial marks if only one element is identified.
Section 3

Speaking — Question list (Part B)

Ask questions one at a time. Remind the student of the spontaneity rule (start within 5 sec, answer within 30 sec) before you begin. Do not reveal the CEFR level of each question.

Q1 — A1

What do you usually do on weekends? Do you prefer to stay home or go out?

Look for: simple present for routines, basic activity vocabulary ("I watch TV / I go shopping"), a simple preference with a one-word reason ("because it's fun").

Q2 — A2

Tell me about a place near your home — a shop, café, or park you like. What is it like and why do you go there?

Look for: descriptive adjectives, location language ("it's near / next to"), present simple for habits, a reason using "because" or "so."

Q3 — B1

Describe a time when something went wrong in your daily routine — maybe you missed a bus, lost something, or had an unexpected problem. What happened and how did you deal with it?

Look for: past tenses (simple + perfect), cause-and-effect ("so I had to…", "because of that…"), ability to narrate a short sequence of events with a resolution.

Q4 — B2

Some people think that technology — smartphones, delivery apps, online shopping — has made daily life easier but less social. Do you agree? Give reasons and consider the other side.

Look for: presenting a viewpoint, acknowledging counter-arguments ("on the other hand…", "while it is true that…"), hedging language ("it could be argued…", "tends to…"), coherent logical structure.

Evaluation

Speaking — Assessment rubric

Score each criterion 1–3. Use overall impression for Part A and per-question scoring for Part B.

Criterion 1 — Needs support 2 — Functional 3 — Confident
Vocabulary Very limited; relies on isolated words or heavy repetition Adequate for the task; occasional gaps but message is clear Wide and natural range; uses precise or idiomatic expressions
Grammar Frequent errors impede understanding Some errors but mostly correct; uses basic structures Few errors; uses varied structures (conditionals, perfect tenses) appropriately
Fluency Long pauses; halting, fragmented speech Occasional hesitation but generally maintains flow Smooth, natural rhythm; no significant pauses or restarts
Pronunciation Frequently difficult to understand; strong L1 interference Generally clear; occasional errors don't block meaning Clear and natural; stress and intonation aid communication
Scoring

Overall level determination guide

Use this holistically across all three sections. One weak section alone should not automatically lower the overall result.

A1

Answers Q1 correctly. Gets the main idea of Script 1. Uses simple words/phrases for the photo. Answers Speaking Q1 with a basic sentence.

A2

Also answers Q2 correctly. Captures both details in Script 1. Describes 3–4 market elements. Uses adjectives and location language in Speaking Q2.

B1

Also answers Q3 correctly. Identifies cause OR solution in Script 2. Narrates a story with past tenses in Speaking Q3. Full photo description with inferred emotion.

B2

Also answers Q4 correctly. Captures both cause AND solution in Script 2. Argues with counter-argument in Speaking Q4. Speculative and cohesive photo description.

Note: If a student scores consistently at one level across all sections, assign that level. If there is a gap of more than one level between sections, record the discrepancy and recommend targeted practice for the weaker skill area.
